## Why the market changes the answer

The same product at the same nominal price can be a good buy or a bad one depending on who is
selling it, whether the warranty will be honoured, and whether the unit was meant for this market.
None of that is visible in a price comparison, and all of it decides what the buyer actually gets.

This is why the market question comes first in the intake. Answering it wrong does not make the
recommendation slightly off — it makes it about a different product.

## Who is actually selling

On Amazon.in and Flipkart the marketplace is not the seller. Check:

- **Seller name and rating.** Brand-owned or authorised distributor is the safe case. Established
  high-volume sellers are usually fine. A new seller with a perfect rating and an unusually low
  price is the case to avoid.
- **Flipkart's own fulfilment** and **Amazon's "sold by" line** — both change the return and
  replacement path materially.
- **Whether the brand lists an authorised online seller list.** Several do, and a purchase from
  outside it can be refused warranty service.
- **Open-box and refurbished labels.** Legitimate, sometimes excellent value, and the warranty
  terms differ from new — read them rather than assuming.

For anything expensive, buying from the brand's own online store or a large offline chain often
costs slightly more and removes the entire seller question. Say so when the gap is small.

## Warranty and the GST invoice

The practical detail that catches people:

- **Manufacturer warranty service usually requires a proper tax invoice** with GST details.
  Marketplace purchases from unauthorised sellers may produce an invoice a service centre will not
  accept.
- **Ask for the invoice before you need it.** Download and keep it at purchase, not when the
  product fails.
- **Some brands register warranty from the date of first activation**, others from invoice date.
  Worth knowing for anything bought ahead of use.
- **Business buyers** need the GSTIN on the invoice to claim input credit, and that has to be
  entered at purchase, not added later.

When comparing a cheaper marketplace listing against a slightly costlier authorised one, the
warranty difference is frequently worth more than the price gap. State it as a number where you
can.

## Grey imports

Common on phones, cameras, audio and laptops, and the usual explanation for a price that looks too
good:

- Units meant for another market, brought in outside the official channel.
- **No India warranty.** The brand's local service centre will decline it, and the seller's
  "replacement warranty" depends entirely on the seller still existing.
- Possible practical issues: charger and plug standard, band support on phones, regional locks,
  no local software or service support.
- Frequently sold as "international model" or with vague warranty language. Absence of a clear
  India warranty statement is the tell.

A meaningful discount on a current-model branded product from an unknown seller is usually this.
It is not always a bad buy — for someone who genuinely does not care about warranty it can be
rational — but it must be a decision rather than a surprise.

## Where to compare

- **Amazon.in and Flipkart** for breadth, and for the seller question above.
- **Brand India stores** for authoritative price and current model, and for confirming what the
  official warranty is.
- **Large offline chains** — Croma, Reliance Digital, Vijay Sales — worth checking, since they run
  their own promotions and price-match sometimes. For large appliances the installation and
  service experience is often better.
- **Price-history trackers** for the marketplaces, to test whether a "discount" is real.
- **Local dealers** for appliances and vehicles, where the negotiated price is frequently below
  any listed one and rarely appears online.

Do not present a marketplace price as *the* price without checking at least one other channel.

## Sale cycles

Genuinely large in India, and worth timing for:

- **The festive season, roughly September into October** — Big Billion Days, Great Indian Festival
  and equivalents. The deepest discounting of the year on electronics and appliances, and
  especially on the outgoing generation.
- **Republic Day sales in January** — the second tier.
- **End of financial year, March** — vehicles and some appliances.
- **New-model launches.** The previous generation drops hard, and this is frequently the best
  value moment in the whole year for a category.

If a window is close, name it, say what the product or its category did last time if you can check
it, and let the user decide. A vague "prices might drop" is not advice.

## EMI, exchange and bank offers

- **No-cost EMI** is not free. The interest is typically built into the price, and a cash purchase
  sometimes attracts a discount of comparable size. Compare total outlay both ways.
- **Exchange offers** bundle a trade-in. Check what the old device fetches sold privately — often
  more, sometimes considerably.
- **Bank card discounts** are real but conditional on the card, a minimum spend and a cap. Confirm
  the user holds the card before counting it in the price.
- **Coupon stacking** varies by platform and changes often. Verify at checkout rather than
  assuming.

These are financing and marketing rather than traps, but they make totals look smaller than they
are, so compare on the final amount paid.

## Service coverage

Underweighted and frequently decisive:

- **Does the brand have a service centre in the user's city?** For appliances, vehicles and
  anything large, a brand with no local presence is a materially worse product whatever the
  reviews say.
- **On-site versus carry-in** for large items.
- **Parts availability** for older models, which is where some cheaper brands fall down two years
  in.
- **Installation** for ACs, washing machines and TVs — who does it, what it costs, and whether
  poor installation voids anything.

Ask the city when the category makes this matter.

## Other markets

**United States** — Amazon plus big-box chains, generally strong return windows, price matching
common, restocking fees on some categories, sales tax varies by state and is added at checkout.
Black Friday is the main cycle.

**United Kingdom and EU** — statutory consumer rights are stronger than the retailer's stated
policy and worth mentioning; distance-selling rules give a return right on online purchases. VAT
is included in displayed prices.

**UAE and Gulf** — official versus parallel imports matter much as in India, and regional warranty
terms vary.

For any market not covered, the transferable checks are the same: who is the seller, does the
warranty apply locally, is the discount measured against a real price, and what does the return
path actually cost.
