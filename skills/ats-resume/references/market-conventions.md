# Resume conventions by market

Read once the candidate has named the market they are applying in. Ask rather than assume — the
differences below change what goes in the file, and getting it wrong is visible immediately to a
local recruiter.

The parsing rules in `rubric.md` are universal. What varies is content: what is expected, what is
optional, and what actively harms an application.

## India

The most common starting point for this skill, and the one where the biggest gap sits between
local convention and what multinational ATS-driven hiring expects.

Frequently present and worth removing for any company hiring through an ATS — Indian offices of
multinationals, funded startups, and every overseas application:

- **Date of birth, marital status, father's or mother's name, nationality, gender, religion,
  caste, passport number, Aadhaar.** Standard on a traditional Indian CV, and they are noise in a
  parser and a liability for an employer bound by anti-discrimination rules.
- **The declaration block.** "I hereby declare that the above information is true to the best of
  my knowledge", often with a place, date and signature line. A legacy convention that adds
  nothing.
- **A photograph.** Not customary in ATS-driven hiring and a reason some employers discard the
  file outright.

Handle this with care rather than by decree. These conventions are still expected by parts of the
market — many PSUs, government roles, some traditional family-run firms, and certain campus
placement processes — and a candidate who has been told by their college placement cell to
include them is not wrong about their context. Explain who expects what, and let them choose. If
they are applying to both, two versions is a reasonable answer.

Worth keeping and getting right:

- **Percentage or CGPA** for recent graduates — genuinely used as a filter for entry-level roles.
  State the scale: `CGPA 8.7/10`, `82%`. Drop it entirely after roughly five years of experience.
- **Notice period** — 30, 60 or 90 days materially affects hiring decisions here and recruiters
  ask early. A line in the summary or contact block saves a round trip.
- **Institution names spelled out.** `National Institute of Technology, Tiruchirappalli (NIT
  Trichy)`, because search matches strings and both forms circulate.
- **City with state or region**: `Bengaluru, Karnataka` or `Bengaluru, India`.

Phone numbers: write the international form, `+91 98765 43210`. A bare 10-digit number is
ambiguous to a recruiter searching across countries.

Currency: state the unit, since lakh and crore do not travel. `Managed a budget of INR 2.4 crore
(approximately USD 290,000)` if the audience may be overseas.

## United States and Canada

- **No photo, no date of birth, no marital status, no gender, no nationality.** Employers here
  will often discard a resume carrying them, because retaining that information before an offer
  creates a compliance exposure. This is the strictest market on the point.
- **"Resume", one page** for under roughly ten years of experience, two beyond that. The one-page
  rule is a human convention, not a parser constraint — say so if a candidate is mutilating real
  content to obey it.
- **No graduation years** if the candidate is worried about age screening; degree and institution
  alone are normal and unremarkable.
- **GPA** only if recent and strong (above about 3.5/4.0). Drop after a few years.
- **US spelling and US date order.** `March 2022`, not `2022 March`. Letter page size.
- **Work authorisation** stated plainly if it is not obvious and does not require sponsorship;
  many portals ask anyway.
- Canada follows the US closely. Quebec applications may expect French; ask.

## United Kingdom and Ireland

- Called a **CV**, and two pages is standard and expected — a one-page CV can read as thin for an
  experienced candidate.
- **No photo, no date of birth, no marital status.** Same reasoning as the US.
- Personal interests are more common and more tolerated than in the US, though they still earn
  their space or should go.
- **UK spelling** — "organised", "programme", "analyse" — and A4 page size.
- References: "References available on request" is a dead line; omit it. Nobody assumes otherwise.
- Grades: degree classification is used and understood — `BSc Computer Science, 2:1`.

## Continental Europe

Varies more by country than any other region, so ask specifically rather than treating "Europe"
as one market.

- **Germany, Austria, Switzerland**: a photo is still common and often expected, as are date of
  birth and nationality, though this is shifting in larger and international employers. A
  *Lebenslauf* is conventionally reverse-chronological and factual, and applications often expect
  certificates (*Arbeitszeugnisse*) attached. If they are applying to a Berlin startup hiring in
  English, the US conventions usually apply instead — ask which.
- **France**: a *CV* of one page is the norm for most candidates, photo optional and common.
- **Netherlands, Nordics**: closer to the UK. Photo optional and drifting out of use.
- **Spain, Italy, Portugal**: photo and personal details still common.
- **Europass**: some public-sector and EU-institution applications require this specific template.
  It is table-heavy and parses poorly, but if it is mandated it is mandated — do not fight the
  requirement, and tell the candidate why the file is weaker than it looks.
- A2 page size is A4 throughout; write dates unambiguously (`March 2022`, not `03/22`).

## Gulf states

UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman, Bahrain.

- Photo, date of birth and nationality are commonly expected — nationality in particular, because
  visa eligibility is part of the hiring decision.
- **Visa status and availability** are material: current visa type, transferability, notice
  period.
- Many roles are filled through recruitment agencies whose own systems are stricter than the
  employer's; the plainest possible formatting matters more here, not less.
- Marital status and number of dependants appear on local templates, tied to family-status
  allowances. Optional; let the candidate decide.

## Australia and New Zealand

- **Resume or CV**, both used, two to three pages is acceptable and normal.
- No photo, no date of birth.
- Referees are more commonly listed directly on the document than in the UK or US, often with
  names and contact details. Ask whether they have permission from those people.
- Government and public-sector applications frequently require **responses to selection criteria**
  as a separate document. That is a different artefact from the resume; flag it rather than trying
  to fold it in.
- Australian spelling, A4.

## Applying across markets

When a candidate is applying to several countries, keep one master version and cut per market.
The history is identical; what changes is which personal details appear, spelling, page size, and
length.

The safe intersection, if they will only maintain one document: no photo, no date of birth, no
marital status, no declaration, international phone format, spelled-out institution names,
unambiguous month-year dates, A4 unless the target is the US or Canada. That version is
acceptable everywhere and optimal in the English-speaking markets — the only places it costs
anything are the German-speaking and Gulf markets where a photo is expected, and it costs less
there than the reverse mistake costs in the US.
