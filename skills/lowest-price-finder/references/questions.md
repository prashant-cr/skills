# The intake

What to ask, what to do with each answer, and when to stop asking.

The test for whether a question earns its place is simple: **would a different
answer change the recommendation?** If not, assume a default and move on. Most
buying questions people are asked online fail that test, which is why nobody
answers them.

## The six that always matter

### 1. Market and location

Decides which stores exist, what shipping costs, whether duty applies, and
often the list price itself. Without it you cannot start.

A rough location is usually enough. Ask for a postcode only when it genuinely
changes the answer — heavy or bulky items, grocery and pharmacy, same-day
delivery, or installation services.

**Default if unanswered:** infer from currency, language, the stores the user
mentions, or earlier conversation. State the assumption in the report.

### 2. Cards, wallets and memberships

The highest-value question in the whole intake, and the one nobody thinks to
ask. In India, instant bank discounts of 5–10% on a specific card are routine
and often exceed every difference between stores. Elsewhere, memberships change
shipping cost: Prime, Flipkart Plus, Walmart+, store loyalty tiers.

**An offer the buyer cannot use is not a discount.** A published "best price"
built on an Axis Bank offer is simply wrong for someone holding only HDFC, and
this is the most common reason a price comparison misleads the person reading
it.

Watch the terms, because the headline is rarely the number:
- **Cap.** "10% off up to ₹1,000" on a ₹50,000 purchase is 2%, not 10%.
- **Minimum spend.**
- **Credit vs debit vs EMI** — frequently different offers on the same card.
- **Once per card per month** — matters for multiple purchases.

**Default if unanswered:** rank on prices with no card offer applied, and list
what each store *would* give with which card. That is genuinely useful and
commits to nothing.

### 3. Condition acceptable

New, open-box, refurbished, or used. Often the single largest saving available,
and purely a preference — never assume it silently in either direction.

Manufacturer-refurbished with warranty is a different proposition from
seller-refurbished with none, and worth distinguishing when it comes up.

**Default if unanswered:** new only, stated as an assumption, with a line
noting what refurbished would save if they are open to it.

### 4. Deadline

Decides whether slow sellers are eligible and whether "wait for the sale" is
even on the table. A ₹2,000 saving is irrelevant if the gift is for Saturday.

**Default if unanswered:** assume no urgency, but flag the delivery date of the
winner so a deadline problem is visible.

### 5. Warranty requirement

The dividing line between the official market and the grey one, and the
explanation for most suspiciously cheap listings. An imported unit with no
local service is genuinely cheaper and genuinely worse; some buyers accept that
knowingly, which is fine — unknowingly is not.

Matters most for electronics, appliances and anything with a service network.
Largely irrelevant for consumables, books and clothing.

**Default if unanswered:** require manufacturer warranty for anything
electronic or mechanical above a trivial price, and say so.

### 6. Stores to prefer or avoid

Some buyers will not use a particular marketplace, already hold credit
somewhere, or want the brand store for service. Cheap and unusable is not
cheap.

**Default if unanswered:** no constraint, but prefer the brand's own store when
it ties on price, since warranty claims are easier there.

## Category-specific questions worth adding

Only when the category is in play:

- **Phones, laptops:** exact storage and RAM configuration; region variant
  (band support, charger type, warranty validity); whether an exchange or
  trade-in is available, which often beats every discount
- **Appliances:** installation included, old-unit removal, capacity, whether
  the price includes delivery to floor
- **Clothing, shoes:** size availability is the real constraint — the cheapest
  store is irrelevant without the size, so check stock before ranking
- **Grocery, consumables:** unit price and pack size, expiry dating,
  subscription discounts
- **Books, media:** edition and format, since ISBNs differ by binding
- **Anything bulky:** shipping is often the largest single variable

## How to ask

Ask **once, grouped, with defaults offered** so the user can reply with a word
or skip entirely:

> Quick questions so I price this properly:
> 1. Which country/city are you buying in?
> 2. Any bank cards or memberships (Prime, Flipkart Plus)? Card offers often
>    beat the price difference between stores.
> 3. New only, or is refurbished/open-box fine?
> 4. Need it by a particular date?
>
> If you'd rather not answer, I'll assume India, new only, no card offers, no
> deadline — and say so in the results.

Then **proceed on the assumptions**. Delivering a comparison with visible
assumptions beats waiting: the user corrects one line and you re-rank in
seconds, and they got something useful immediately.

## When not to ask at all

- The user pasted a link and asked "is this a good price?" — they want a yes or
  no with evidence. Price it, compare it, answer. Ask nothing up front.
- The item is cheap enough that the whole spread is small. Nobody wants an
  interview to buy a phone case.
- The conversation already answered it. Re-asking reads as not listening.
