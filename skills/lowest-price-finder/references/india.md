## Bank offers

Frequently the largest single lever on the final price, and the reason asking
which cards the buyer holds matters more here than anywhere else.

Typical shape: "10% instant discount with HDFC Bank Credit Cards and EMI
transactions, up to ₹1,500, minimum purchase ₹5,000."

Read the terms, because the headline is usually not the number:

- **The cap dominates on expensive items.** 10% up to ₹1,500 on a ₹60,000
  laptop is 2.5%, not 10%. On a ₹15,000 phone it is the full 10%.
- **Credit, debit and EMI are frequently different offers** on the same bank —
  EMI often carries the better rate.
- **No-cost EMI is not always free.** Interest is sometimes discounted from the
  price up front, so the cash price and the EMI price differ; and processing
  fees may apply. Compare the total paid, not the monthly figure.
- **Usually once per card per month**, occasionally once per statement period.
- Offers change between sale events and between the two big marketplaces, which
  deliberately stagger them.

If the buyer has not said which cards they hold, rank without card offers and
list what each store would give with which card. That is honest and still
useful.

## The sale calendar

Indian e-commerce discounting is concentrated in events far more than in
day-to-day pricing, so timing frequently beats store choice.

- **Festive season (roughly Sept–Nov)** — Flipkart Big Billion Days and Amazon
  Great Indian Festival, generally overlapping around Navratri through Diwali.
  The deepest discounts of the year on electronics and appliances.
- **Republic Day (late January)** and **Independence Day (mid-August)** — the
  next tier down, strong on electronics.
- **End of financial year (March)** — good for appliances and vehicles as
  dealers clear stock.
- **Prime Day / Flipkart tier-member events** — mid-year, membership-gated.
- **Regional festivals** — Onam, Pongal, Durga Puja drive genuine local
  discounting.

Practical rule: for a discretionary electronics purchase within about six weeks
of a major event, saying "wait" is often worth more than any store comparison.
For anything needed now, ignore this entirely and say so.

Treat launch-day "deals" sceptically — a new model's introductory price is
rarely its lowest, and the outgoing model usually falls further.

## Exchange and trade-in

Both large marketplaces offer exchange on phones, laptops and appliances, and
the quoted exchange value routinely exceeds every discount under discussion. It
is worth asking about for any device replacement.

Caveats worth flagging: the quote is provisional and reassessed by the pickup
agent, downgrades at the door are common, and the offered value is usually
below what a private sale would fetch. Convenience against price, and the buyer
should make that trade knowingly.

## GST invoice

For a business buyer, a proper GST invoice allows input tax credit, which is
effectively an 18% reduction on most electronics. This can dwarf every other
consideration.

Ask whether the purchase is for a business. If it is, filter to sellers who
provide a GST invoice — both marketplaces have a business-buyer mode. Grey-
market sellers generally cannot, which resolves the choice on its own.

## Grey imports

Common in phones, cameras, watches, audio and cosmetics, and the explanation
for most implausibly cheap Indian listings.

Signals: "imported", "international variant", "seller warranty" rather than
brand warranty, no GST invoice, price well under every authorised dealer,
packaging "may vary".

What the buyer loses: Indian manufacturer service. For phones this can also
mean missing band support and a non-Indian charger. Some brands will not
service an imported unit at any price.

Legitimate to buy knowingly, and never to be ranked against warrantied stock
without saying what it is.

## Marketplace mechanics

**Amazon.in** — check whether the seller is Appario/Cloudtail-style
platform-linked or a third party, and whether it is Fulfilled by Amazon.
FBA means Amazon holds the stock and returns are straightforward.

**Flipkart** — "Flipkart Assured" indicates quality-checked and
platform-fulfilled. F-Assured items are generally safer for returns.

On both, **the seller determines warranty, authenticity and returns**, not the
platform. Report the seller, not just the site. The same product on the same
marketplace at two prices is usually two different sellers with two different
levels of recourse.

Brand stores — Samsung, Apple, Xiaomi, Sony India and others — often match
marketplace prices, run their own bank offers, and make warranty claims
simpler. Worth checking every time and routinely skipped.

## Delivery and payment

- **Pincode determines everything** on availability, delivery date and
  sometimes price. A product can be unavailable a few kilometres away.
- **Cash on delivery** may carry a surcharge and sometimes blocks the bank
  discount, which can silently cost more than the convenience is worth.
- **UPI** is near-universal and occasionally carries its own cashback.
- **Open-box delivery** is available from some sellers on high-value
  electronics and is worth requesting.
- **Return windows are short** — commonly 7 to 10 days, and some categories are
  return-only-if-defective. Check before recommending an unfamiliar seller, as
  the return policy is the real cost of being wrong.
